# Koloss Media: first acquisition test

Audience: business owners and personal brands. These are drafts for review; no messages have been sent and no prospect list has been populated.

## Choose one offer for each campaign

- **Personal-brand content:** Target owners or creators with a clear offer and useful expertise who struggle with consistent production. Route to a scoped Custom content engagement.
- **Search foundation:** Target businesses with a functioning site and a clear offer that need an SEO/content plan. Route to Core only when the scope and budget fit.
- **Paid acquisition:** Target businesses with a proven offer, conversion tracking access, sales capacity, and separate media spend. Route to Scale after qualification.

Use a small initial batch your team can follow up with properly. A suggested first test is 20 carefully selected prospects for one offer. This is an experiment size, not a forecast or promised conversion benchmark.

## Prospect research record

For each prospect, record the public business/profile URL, what they sell, a specific observed marketing gap, why the chosen offer fits, and the relevant decision-maker role. Use the pipeline for stage, owner, next action, and due date. Do not claim to have audited performance or revenue without evidence.

## Qualification and next step

Establish the need, audience/offer, decision maker, service budget, separate media budget, timeline, and client capacity before proposing. If scope is uncertain, use discovery rather than pushing a package. Record exactly one next action and an owner.

## Review the test

Use one prospect cohort and record: prospects approached, responses, qualified leads, calls booked, calls attended, proposals, initial payments collected, acquisition spending, and delivery cost estimates. Record the reasons for rejection or loss. Avoid merging older sales with new lead counts.

Continue a channel when the leads fit, collected revenue supports the costs, and your delivery team can fulfill the work. Change the audience, message, or offer when the evidence identifies a specific mismatch. Increasing volume is a separate decision after that review.

## Measurement hooks in the website

The website emits browser events named `koloss:conversion` for consultation clicks, package-finder use, brief preparation, and calendar opens. Their detail contains only an event name and offer ID. No visitor goal, budget, URL, or free-text response is included.

These hooks do not install analytics, send data, or create a report. An approved analytics implementation can listen to them later. A calendar open is not a completed booking; booking confirmation needs a verified calendar integration or a reconciliation of actual calendar records.
